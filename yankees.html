The New York Yankees

The Legendary History of the Team

The New York Yankees baseball team are based in the Bronx in New York City and were officially named the Yankees in 1913. One of the first major successes in early Yankees baseball history was earning their first American League pennant in 1921. They are highly regarded as one of the most famous teams in MLB, considering they have won 27 World Series titles. Generations of Yankees players are regarded as well-known all-stars, such as Babe Ruth, Lou Gehrig, Mickey Mantle, Joe DiMaggio, Derek Jeter, and Aaron Judge, who bring widescale attention from fans and the media. The biggest rivals the Yankees face are the Boston Red Sox and New York Mets, where these games tend to be filled with intense chatter, cheering, and banter among fans whose common goal is to see their team win.

Root, Root, Root for the Yankees!

The Yankees are best known for their white and navy uniforms, with a fan-favorite uniform choice being the pinstripes. Their beloved ballpark is based in the Bronx in New York City, the birthplace of the team, where many of their loyal fans regularly attend their games during season and participate in their stadium traditions. One Yankees tradition includes the fans singing along to the famous song, "Take Me Out to the Ball Game," written by Jack Norworth and Albert Von Tilzer, performed during the 7th inning stretch, where the line "Root, Root, Root for the...," is filled in with the word "Yankees!" Select players are the face of this baseball franchise, but every member of the team has contributed to the Yankees legacy. This sport requires a great level of skill to be displayed throughout the game by the players in order to create such a loyal and passionate fanbase, which the Yankees have accomplished in building.
