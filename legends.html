Yankees Legends

Some of the greatest players in baseball history have represented the Yankees. A few players to reference include Babe Ruth, Lou Gehrig, Mickey Mantle, Joe DiMaggio, and Derek Jeter. All of these retired and/or late Yankees players mentioned have had their jersey numbers retired by the Yankees franchise, which does not allow any other current or new player to wear that number. These five athletes have made history through their raw talent, continuous drive and work ethic, and respected achievements. All of these athletes have gone on to win awards recognizing their feats and have been inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame for their outstanding careers.

Babe Ruth - The Sultan of Swat

Babe Ruth, born February 2nd, 1895 in Baltimore, Maryland, was nicknamed The Sultan of Swat, and played his first season with the New York Yankees in 1920. He soon became a full-time outfielder with the Yankees, and recognized as one of the greatest power hitters. By the end of Ruth's career, his homerun statistic was recorded at 714 total. To acknowledge his achievements, Ruth, along with four other players chosen in the class of 1936, were inducted in the Baseball Hall of Fame. The Sultan of Swat passed in 1948, and the New York Yankees officially retired his jersey number that same year to honor his legacy.

Lou Gehrig - The Iron Horse

Born on June 19th, 1903, in Manhattan, Lou Gehrig would go on to become a beloved Yankees player in his hometown of New York. At the age of 19, Gehrig joined the team during their 1923 season, and became their pinch hitter offensively. As for defense, Gehrig lacked the skill necessary to be a primary position player, so he was placed as a first baseman, a position that often allows the player to perform simple defensive plays to bat in the lineup. He was awarded the nickname The Iron Horse due to his participation in 2,130 consecutive games, which broke the record for most consecutive games played. Despite his accomplishments, Gehrig was diagnosed with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) in 1939, and the Yankees announced his election in the Hall of Fame that same year, along with his retirement. After these events, the Yankees paid an urgent tribute to honor Gehrig by retiring his jersey number, making him the first player in MLB to have his number retired.

Mickey Mantle - The Commerce Comet

Mickey Mantle was conditioned from a young age to become the professional baseball player he was. His father named him after Mickey Cochrane, a Hall of Fame player in hopes that he would one day follow that very path. However, duing his youth, Mantle suffered an infection called osetomyelitis from an injury to his shin, which is speculated to be a cause of his later injuries. Mickey Mantle's entire professional baseball career took place with the New York Yankees, and he made his first debut with this team in 1951. As a switch-hitter, Mantle was able to swing as both a left- and right-handed batter, and combined with his speed, his plate appearances were unpredictable for the pitchers he faced, allowing him to dominate. Mantle would soon become the Most Valuable Player in 1957 and be awarded the Gold Glove in 1962 for his performances in center field, and inducted into the Hall of Fame in 1974.

Joe DiMaggio - The Yankee Clipper

Joseph Paul DiMaggio was born on November 25th, 1914, in Martinez, California, and was better known as "The Yankee Clipper." However, this famous Yankees player introduced himself to the game of baseball by going to local sandlots in order to avoid going fishing or completing chores. The simple fact is, it wasn't the sport that appealed DiMaggio, it was the opportunity to miss his usual boat work. Making his debut with the Yankees in 1936 primarily as a center fielder, the rookie proved to be a reliable asset for the team, and was named Most Valuable Player in 1939, shortly after his debut. He is most remembered for his record set in 1941 of executing at least one hit for 56 consecutive games, which has yet to be broken by any other MLB player. Considering his successful career and quick rise to popularity with the Yankees fan base, DiMaggio is considered one of the greats of all time. During 1951, he officially retired after suffering a heel injury a few years prior. To honor his legacy, he was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1955.

Derek Jeter - The Captain

Derek Jeter, born on June 26, 1974, joined the Yankees franchise on June 1, 1992, when he was drafted in the first round as the 6th overall pick. Through the draft, Jeter was an excellent prospect, making him a wanted player by many teams, which made it surprising to the Yankees when he was still available after the first few picks. The rookie later made his debut at the age of 20, in 1995 as a shortstop, which is a key defensive position, also known as one of the middle positions. The following year after his debut, in 1996, Jeter was awarded the Baseball America Rookie of the Year Award. He played his entire career with the Yankees, lasting from 1995-2014, to which over those two decades of professional baseball, he won 5 World Series titles, and was awarded the World Series Most Valuable Player. Due to such performances, Jeter was elected to the Hall of Fame in 2020, however, due to the circumstances of the COVID-19 pandemic, he was officially inducted in 2021.

Their Lasting Legacies

Babe Ruth, Lou Gehrig, Mickey Mantle, Joe DiMaggio, and Derek Jeter were prominent figures in buiding and maintaning the legacy of Yankees baseball, and remain fan-favorites among the fan base. Despite no longer being a part of the lineup, their best moments will always be cherished and remembered. Many of these memories come from game clutching homeruns or foul balls that fly into the stands where fans get lucky enough to catch a ball in the stadium. Still today, these legends are honored through many World Series pennants, signed jerseys and baseballs, and loyal fans.
