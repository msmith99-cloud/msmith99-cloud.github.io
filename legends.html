Yankees Legends

Some of the greatest players in baseball history have represented the Yankees. A few players to reference include Babe Ruth, Lou Gehrig, Mickey Mantle, Joe DiMaggio, and Derek Jeter. All of these retired and/or late Yankees players mentioned have had their jersey numbers retired by the Yankees franchise, which does not allow any other current or new player to wear that number. These five athletes have made history through their raw talent, continuous drive and work ethic, and respected achievements.

Babe Ruth - The Sultan of Swat

Babe Ruth, born February 2nd, 1895 in Baltimore, Maryland, was nicknamed The Sultan of Swat, and played his first season with the New York Yankees in 1920. He soon became a full-time outfielder and recognized as one of the greatest power hitters. By the end of Ruth's career, his homerun statistic was recorded at 714 total. To acknowledge his achievements, Ruth, along with four other players chosen in the class of 1936, were inducted in the Baseball Hall of Fame. The Sultan of Swat passed in 1948, and the New York Yankees officially retired his jersey number that same year to honor his legacy.

Lou Gehrig - The Iron Horse

Born on June 19th, 1903, in Manhattan, Lou Gehrig would go on to become a beloved Yankees player in his hometown of New York. At the age of 19, Gehrig joined the team during their 1923 season, and became their pinch hitter offensively. As for defense, Gehrig lacked the skill necessary to be a primary position player, so he was placed as a first baseman, a position that often allows the player to perform simple defensive plays to bat in the lineup. He was awarded the nickname The Iron Horse due to his participation in 2,130 consecutive games, which broke the record for most consecutive games played. Despite his accomplishments, Gehrig was diagnosed with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) in 1939, and the Yankees announced his retirement alongside their urgent tribute to honor Gehrig by retiring his jersey number.

Mickey Mantle - The Commerce Comet

Mickey Mantle was conditioned from a young age to become the professional baseball player he was. His father named him after Mickey Cochrane, a Hall of Fame player in hopes that he would one day follow that very path. Mickey Mantle's entire professional baseball career took place with the New York Yankees, and he made his first debut with this team in 1951. Mantle would soon become the Most Valuable Player in 1957 and be awarded the Gold Glove in 1962 for his performances, and inducted into the Hall of Fame in 1974.
